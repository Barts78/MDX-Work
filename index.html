Reading the table. Click a row to chart that programme. Apps and Offers are cumulative unique totals. Accepted is every accepted offer, conditional or unconditional, including students who have since paid. Paid is new payments plus IFP-progressing students. Everything responds to the Week, Domicile and level filters.

Conversion rates. App→Offer = offers ÷ applications. Offer→Accept = accepted ÷ offers. Accept→Paid = paid ÷ accepted. App→Paid = paid ÷ applications. Darker shading means a stronger rate within that column.

Target, gap and Δ gap. Targets are held at the latest week's figure for every week, so changes reflect recruitment only. Gap = paid − target. Δ gap is the change in that gap since the "Compare to" week. Foundation targets exist only for the section total, so the IFP rows show "–".

Last year. Paid last yr is the same programme in the matching week of last year's September cycle (52 weeks earlier); vs last yr is the difference. "–" means there is no matching week or programme. Renamed programmes are linked (MSc Applied AI and Data Science = last year's MSc Data Science, plus the Cyber Security and LLM International Business Law renames). MSc Management and MSc Cyber Security and Pen Testing have no equivalent this year. Last year's earlier reports have no total for offers issued, so offer-based last-year figures are unavailable before late August.

Proj. paid. Projected final payments = paid now + expected further payments. The expected part is built from students still in the pipeline: live offers (awaiting a reply, or accepted but not yet paid) plus the share of not-yet-offered applications that went on to receive an offer last year. It is multiplied by a conversion rate taken from the same programme last year: of the students in that pipeline at the same week, how many had paid by last year's final report (3 Oct). Because one year of data per programme is thin, that rate is blended with the Foundation / UG / PG average, and small programmes lean more on the section average. A * means there is no equivalent programme last year, so the section rate was used alone. Green means the projection meets target; red means it is below 90% of target. At the latest week it equals actual payments. When tested against last year's cycle it ran a little high early on and settled as the cycle progressed, so treat early-cycle figures as indicative, not a forecast to commit to.

Entry filter. Year 2 and Year 3 rows are direct entries into later years of a degree. By default they are included in their parent programme. Year 1 only removes them from every figure on the page (cards, charts, table, projections, last-year comparisons), and Year 2/3 direct entry only shows just those students. Targets cover Year 1 entry only, so Year 1 only is the like-for-like view against target; with direct entry included, gaps and projections run ahead of what the targets measure. Programmes that are mostly direct entry shrink sharply under Year 1 only; Information Technology, for example, is almost entirely Year 3. How programmes are grouped. Year 2 / Year 3 rows are rolled into their parent programme. The MBA variants are combined. Foundation programmes were renamed on 4 Sep and are matched in order (Business, Law, Computer Science, Psychology, Arts) and shown as "IFP:" rows, which are the international foundation pathways, not the full degree programmes of the same name. Programmes sit under their level total.
